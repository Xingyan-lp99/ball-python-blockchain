<p><a href="https://material.io/blog/inclusive-imagery-at-google">Inclusive imagery is important to me</a> as an art director, and thinking about ways to evolve the conversation around that topic keeps my work innovative and interesting. So it was very compelling when I was told we were creating realistic names and backstories for <a href="http://material.io/blog/asset-people-1">&ldquo;real&rdquo; people</a> whose lives would help illustrate different interface scenarios <a href="https://m3.material.io/">throughout our Material 3 guidelines</a>. They were all going to need curated, individualized images that weren&rsquo;t selected at random for generic, placeholder characters like Johnny Appleseed at 123 Main Street.</p>
<p>It was an exciting opportunity and also one that came with unique challenges: Building a design system like M3 takes time and illustrating the system, in this case, was a final step. When we reached this point in the process, it was too short of a turn-around to commission photography and illustration, but I still needed to craft a believable, visual narrative for each new profile, so I turned to stock agencies.</p>
<p>The first step was to decide which agencies to work with. Because our guidelines are available to a global audience, and it is important that the audience feel represented, I decided to draw from a stock agency that works with photographers and artists from all over the world.</p>
<p>From there, another challenge surfaced. Stock imagery is extremely convenient, and more affordable than commissioning custom art, but in using stock I am limited to what&rsquo;s available in their libraries. I needed to figure out a way to create genuine and cohesive depictions of people with prefabricated options.</p>
<p>My goal in art direction is to create a feeling of connection throughout a body of work by producing imagery with a consistent look and feel. As I started working through each profile, I began to realize the benefit of using stock imagery &ndash; it was the variety of lighting, styles, and subjects that made these collections believable.</p>
<p>Visual representation of life doesn't have to be overly polished. Perfect visual cohesion is not always realistic. Instead of thinking of everything being coordinated, I imagined how specific images would surface in each person&rsquo;s life. From there, I was able to create convincing profiles.</p>
<h2>Case study: Sam Pietersen</h2>
<p><a href="https://material.io/blog/asset-people-1">To start building Sam&rsquo;s identity</a>, who uses they/them pronouns, I started with their profile image. Knowing they are shy and reserved, I chose portraits with a soft focus to respect that part of them and their privacy while also elevating their shyness to a creative level.</p>
<p>&lt;mio-image index=0&gt;&lt;/mio-image&gt;</p>
<p>Since Sam is a nature enthusiast, I knew their phone&rsquo;s camera roll would be full of outdoor adventures, and it was likely Sam would choose one of those photos for their phone&rsquo;s wallpaper. Getting the wallpaper right was important because it defined what Sam&rsquo;s UI looked like with <a href="https://m3.material.io/styles/color/dynamic-color/user-generated-color">dynamic theming</a>.</p>
<p>&lt;mio-image index=1&gt;&lt;/mio-image&gt;</p>
<p>I focused on nature-based photography because there is a lot to choose from in stock image libraries and they appear the most organic in a user-generated context. If the images were too professional or commercial looking, Sam&rsquo;s life would get lost in the visual narrative.</p>
<p>Before I made the selections for nature imagery, I went on a hike and photographed what I saw &ndash; birds, lakes, trees, moths, and all the mushrooms I could find. The goal in doing this was to make the imagery feel more personal and realistic. If I saw it in my own experience, I knew it was something Sam could also encounter.</p>
<p>Pulling art for the media was my favorite part because it was an opportunity to have a little fun. Because the subject matter and styling of podcasts, albums, and movie posters are all so diverse, I had visual flexibility in building out Sam&rsquo;s media library. I could choose literal, abstract, user-generated, and commercial photography and illustration while still capturing the essence of Sam&rsquo;s identity.</p>
<p>&lt;mio-image index=2&gt;&lt;/mio-image&gt;</p>
<p>Putting people before images helped me bring Sam and the other avatars to life. By respecting them as individuals with personalities and full lives, I was able to capture and represent the nuances of their identities. One aesthetic rarely governs a single perspective, so you can create rich, convincing, and relatable narratives using a variety of stock imagery and still express genuine personalities that real people will recognize and feel they know.</p>
