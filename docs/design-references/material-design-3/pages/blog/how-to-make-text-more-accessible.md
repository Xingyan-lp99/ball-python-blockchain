<h2>Optical Size variable fonts</h2>
<p><a href="https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/optical_sizes" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Optical size</a> in fonts refers to the design adjustments made to a typeface to optimize its legibility and aesthetics across various sizes. The impact of optical size on text accessibility is significant, as it plays a crucial role in enhancing readability and comprehension for individuals with visual impairments or reading difficulties.</p>
<p>Smaller optical sizes feature wider letter <a href="https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/spacing" target="_blank" rel="noopener">spacing</a>, increased stroke contrast, and more open counters to maintain legibility, while larger optical sizes focus on refining details and proportions for aesthetic appeal. By choosing the appropriate optical size for a given context, designers can ensure that text is both visually appealing and easily readable.</p>
<p>Learn more: <a href="https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/choosing_type/choosing_typefaces_that_have_optical_sizes" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Choosing typefaces that have optical sizes</a></p>
<p>To find a font that has an optical size axis, go to the <a href="https://fonts.google.com/variablefonts" target="_blank" rel="noopener">variable fonts page</a> and follow these steps:</p>
<p>Step 1: Select &ldquo;opsz&rdquo; in the dropdown menu.</p>
<p>Step 2:&nbsp; Scroll through the list of available fonts and select one.</p>
<p>Step 3: Go to the Type Tester and see how the selected text changes with and without the optical size axis.</p>
