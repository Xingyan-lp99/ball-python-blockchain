<p>Derek Brahney is a multidisciplinary artist based in New York, working across sculpture, drawing, photography, and conceptual illustration. Brahney is the latest artist and collaborator on The Avatar Project.&nbsp; The Avatar Project provides Google designers and engineers with a variety of images, both photography and illustration, that visibly embody Google's core values around diversity and inclusion. Derek&rsquo;s brief was to bring his style and ideas to what conceptual identity means in representation. After working with Derek for a few months on The Avatar Project, we sat down together for a more personal conversation about the project and his process.</p>
<p>&lt;mio-image index=0&gt;&lt;/mio-image&gt;</p>
<p><strong>Hi Derek, thanks so much for working on this project and for taking the time to do this interview. Could you please tell us a little about yourself?</strong></p>
<p>Hi, my name is Derek Brahney (pronounced [Brain-NEE] not [BRAh-NEE]), I&rsquo;m an artist based in New York. I have a background in industrial design and if I were to describe my work in one sentence I would say I like to explore the poetic potential of objects.</p>
<p><strong>Haha, your last name sounds pretty fitting. Okay, let&rsquo;s start easy. What are you watching, reading or listening to?</strong></p>
<p>Watching: <a href="https://www.youtube.com/c/thelouisianachannel" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The Louisiana Channel on YouTube</a> &ndash; Reading: <a href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/30659.Meditations" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Meditations</a> by Marcus Aurelius &ndash; Listening to: Ambient playlists tailored expressly to my liking via algorithm</p>
<p><strong>Did anything unusual or unexpected happen today?</strong></p>
<p>There is an older, very successful artist whose work I admire that lives in my neighborhood and also has my same initials, D.B. We&rsquo;ve never met but always manage to be in the same places at the same time. This morning he ended up standing behind me in line in a coffee shop, so I finally introduced myself and we ended up having a really great 25-minute conversation and are going to meet up for studio visits. These chance interactions are one of the things I love most about New York.</p>
<p><strong>What are some of your favorite places in the world or nyc?</strong></p>
<p>World: London, Paris, Copenhagen, Marrakech, beaches, islands, lakes, mountains</p>
<p>NYC: The Met, Balthazar, Ear Inn</p>
<p>&lt;mio-image index=1&gt;&lt;/mio-image&gt;</p>
<p><strong>Any hobbies?</strong></p>
<p>Getting out into nature: surfing, camping, hiking, anything along those lines, wandering new cities for the first time.</p>
<p><strong>Okay, last intro question - What would you be if you weren&rsquo;t an artist?</strong></p>
<p>I truly don&rsquo;t know.</p>
<p><strong>Let&rsquo;s talk about your art, do you consider your work to be photography, illustration or sculpture?&nbsp;</strong></p>
<p>My work often blurs mediums, even if I try to stick to a certain approach I always find myself introducing other elements. I&rsquo;ve never been concerned with categorizing what I do, it&rsquo;s the space in between categories that I find most exciting and natural to operate in.</p>
<p><strong>The work is so fun and thoughtful. What does being a conceptual artist mean to you?</strong></p>
<p>1 + 1 = ?</p>
<p>&lt;mio-image index=2&gt;&lt;/mio-image&gt;</p>
<p><strong>What were your inspirations for this shoot?</strong></p>
<p>Everyday life, everyday objects and how these objects can act as symbols for complex ideas when presented in a certain way.</p>
<p><strong>Let&rsquo;s go back to you for a second. What is your favorite artwork/who is your favorite artist?</strong></p>
<p>There are quite a few but if I had to choose I&rsquo;d probably say the artist I most frequently return to is Magritte.</p>
<p><strong>If you could own one piece of art what would it be?</strong></p>
<p>Salvator Mundi by Leonardo Da Vinci, currently the most valuable painting in the world at $450 million. I would sell it and then I would have enough money to buy a two bedroom apartment in New York City.</p>
<p><strong>What about trends? Which current art world trends are you following?</strong></p>
<p>I keep up with what&rsquo;s going on, however when it comes to my own work I have to try especially hard to block all that out. The goal is always to get to the core of what I want to say and how I want to say it, and paying too much attention to trends can very much be a distraction in that way. Of course, we don&rsquo;t work in a vacuum and what I see and hear will find its way into the work in many different ways.</p>
<p>&lt;mio-image index=3&gt;&lt;/mio-image&gt;</p>
<p><strong>What do you want people to know about the collection?</strong></p>
<p>I don&rsquo;t know how to 3D render, these images are all of handmade physical models that I built and photographed.</p>
<p><strong>What do you want this collection to say?</strong></p>
<p>My hope is that this collection of images states (in a clear and direct way) the ambiguous, if that makes sense. The brief for this project involved representing &lsquo;conceptual identity&rsquo;, and I think the most successful images are the ones that involve something very familiar while remaining open to interpretation.</p>
<p><strong>Does your work comment on current social or political issues?</strong></p>
<p>I like to view my images as a sort of prompt for the viewer. When dealing with social, political, or any issues, I prefer not to say or declare overtly, but just to suggest or hint at an idea as if planting a seed in the mind.</p>
<p><strong>Anything else you want to add?&nbsp;</strong></p>
<p>This was a challenging and fun project to work on and I hope the images inspire curiosity and make a few people look twice and smile.</p>
