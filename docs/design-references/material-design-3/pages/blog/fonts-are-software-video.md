<p>If you were a web designer in the early days of the Internet, you might remember that there were few &ldquo;web safe&rdquo; typefaces, such as Arial and Georgia. As a result, many websites looked similar. To use a new typeface, you had to embed small Flash files for each heading in your layout.</p>
<p>Now with <a href="https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/web_font" target="_blank" rel="noopener">web fonts</a> and <a href="https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/using_variable_fonts_on_the_web" target="_blank" rel="noopener">variable fonts</a>, the situation has changed dramatically. Designers have many more typeface choices to customize their websites and brands.</p>
<p>Here are some of the ways that variable fonts are changing type:</p>
<ul>
<li>They allow designers to create more nuanced and expressive typography with different weights, widths, and other axes.</li>
<li>They can improve the performance of web pages by reducing the number of font files that need to be loaded.</li>
</ul>
<p>Tobias Kunisch went from being a webmaster at Google in 2010 to being one of the co-founders of Google Fonts, whose mission is to provide open source fonts without charge. Kunisch spoke at the <a href="https://config.figma.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Figma Config Conference</a> in June 2023 in San Francisco about how fonts are like software and how they open up new digital design possibilities.</p>
