## Designing icons

<h2 id="isPasted">Design principles</h2><p>Icons are an essential element of any interface, packing an informative punch into a small form factor. They’re designed to be simple, modern, friendly, and sometimes quirky. To ensure consistency and readability, their limited size means that each icon must strictly adhere to guidance while still expressing essential characteristics. </p>

Front view of boat icon.

<p>Simplify icons for greater clarity and legibility</p>

<h3 id="isPasted">Additional optical icon sizes</h3><p>Icons support additional sizes: 20dp, 40dp, and 48dp, with 20dp primarily for desktop, dense layouts, and small scale visuals, and 40dp and 48dp optimized for display or headline type, plus larger screen sizes.  </p>

No parts of the icon should extend outside of the trim area.</p>

## Applying icons

<h2 id="isPasted">Icon &amp; Material Symbol styles</h2>

<p>Material Symbols are the new default, and are available in three styles: <strong>outlined, rounded, </strong>and<strong> sharp</strong>. (The legacy Material Icons continue to be available, but don’t have the variable font capabilities of Material Symbols.)</p>

<h3 id="isPasted">Outlined style </h3><p>Outlined symbols use stroke and fill attributes for a light, clean style that works well in dense UIs. The stroke weight of outlined icons can be adjusted to complement or contrast the weight of your typography.</p>

<h2 id="isPasted">Customizing Symbols</h2>

<p>Material Symbols have four adjustable stylistic variable font attributes called <strong>axes</strong>. An axis is a typographic term referring to the attribute of a symbol that can be altered to create visual variations. </p><p>Each style symbol contains four axes: <strong>weight, fill, grade,</strong> and <strong>optical size</strong>.</p>

<h3 id="isPasted">Grade</h3><p>Weight and grade affect a symbol’s thickness. Adjustments to grade are more granular than adjustments to weight and have a smaller impact on the size of the symbol.</p><p>Grade is also available in some text fonts. Grade levels between text and symbols can be matched for a harmonious visual effect. For example, if the text font has a -25 grade value, the symbols can match it with a suitable value of -25. </p>
